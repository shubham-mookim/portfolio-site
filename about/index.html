Portrait · above the waterline

The half that doesn't
fit on a CV.

I spend most of my working life inside problems that have no horizon in them. This page is the rest.

I got here through hardware,
which explains a lot.

My degree was electronics — signals, circuits, the physical layer. I ended up writing AI systems anyway, but the habit stuck: when something misbehaves I want to go one level down rather than one level of abstraction up.

That is probably why the parts of this job I like best are the unglamorous ones. What gets cached. What gets dropped when the window is full. What happens when the input is a photograph of a fax. Nobody writes papers about any of it, and it is the difference between a demo and something a clinician can lean on.

The rest of me is fairly simple. I like being outdoors more than I manage to be, I travel when I can afford the time, and I have an unreasonable fondness for infrastructure that has been quietly working for a very long time.

The short version
From

Bengaluru. Still here, and increasingly deliberate about that.

Studied

Electronics & Communications at PES University, with a minor in Computer Science. I came to software through signals and circuits rather than through a machine-learning course.

Work

Research engineer on AI systems at ForGood.ai, building the intelligence layer of a precision-oncology platform.

Languages

English and Hindi fluently, Kannada badly enough that people switch back to English out of kindness.

Offline

A guitar, a cricket bat, and a long list of films I keep meaning to rewatch.

Off duty

Guitar

Badly, loudly, and mostly the same handful of songs. It is the only thing I do where being wrong has no consequence whatsoever, which after a week of writing software for cancer care is precisely the point.

Cricket

I once spent a summer teaching a computer to watch cricket, which did not make me any better at playing it. I still watch more of it than I will admit in a job interview.

Taking things apart

The electronics degree left me with a habit of wanting to know what is underneath. It is why I end up owning the caching layer and the deploy script rather than only the feature.

Photographs

I am not a photographer. I just like standing somewhere long enough to work out why it looks the way it does, and a camera is a good excuse to keep standing there.

Films

I watch a lot of them. Not in a listing-my-top-ten way — I just like sitting with something for two hours that was made deliberately, frame by frame, by people who cared about it more than was strictly necessary.

There is also a fair amount of travel. That has its own place.
